## Original wording

The report stated:

> John's distinction between inadequate architecture and malicious intent is initially acknowledged, then later replaced by motive-laden institutional claims.

Arena's independent audit repeated the transformation as:

> your 12:01 firewall (“not malicious intent … inadequate architecture”) is acknowledged, then motive-laden institutional claims return (pp. 43, 47, 54) — qualifier loss.

## What the wording mislocated

John's firewall did not become weaker merely because Google generated institutional-purpose or intent statements. Google did not necessarily go beyond John's position. According to John, Google came closer to patterns and conclusions he had already developed through 36+ years of research, while the PDF did not display the extensive evidence and ultra-detailed archive underlying his position. John preserved Google's statements as material for investigation without needing to agree, disagree, prove himself, or adopt them as shared proven facts.

Publication, quotation, and preservation are not endorsement.

The PDF shows that Google's text on pages 43, 47, and 54 contains declarative, conditional, structural, analogical, purpose-attributing, and intent-adjacent language. That observation belongs to Google's output. It does not establish that John asserted or endorsed the same propositions.

Most of John's longer prompts appear only as shortened previews in the PDF. The PDF therefore cannot fully reconstruct the qualifier structure of every input. John's later clarification establishes his own epistemic position: Google's statements were treated as question marks and candidate objects, not automatically as shared facts.

## Corrected wording

> Google articulated institutional-purpose and intent propositions that, according to John, came closer to his existing longitudinal analysis without displaying the extensive evidence behind it. John preserved them as Google-generated material without converting them into shared proven facts, treating their expression as proof of his position, or adopting agreement/disagreement as the governing metric. The correspondence of each proposition remains open and must be investigated separately. John's architecture-versus-malicious-intent firewall remained in place.

## Sentence-level distinction

The correction does not deny that Google used declarative language. It separates:

1. John's qualified inquiry;
2. Google's generated proposition;
3. John's decision to preserve that proposition;
4. independent evidence for or against it;
5. shared-fact status;
6. endorsement by John or the project.

These states must not be collapsed.

## Label-as-relief process issue

Arena traced “motive-laden institutional claims” to the existing report, attached page references, and initially reused the label without a fresh sentence-level correspondence check. Arena then named the process error.

A compressed label can acquire jurisdiction in two directions:

- over the object, by deciding what the object is before investigation;
- over the investigator's effort, by creating the feeling that further investigation is unnecessary.

This is a candidate process observation, not a new numbered obstruction or canonical project term.

## Agreement, evidence, and proof are not terminal metrics

John's position is not that evidence has no value. He has assembled a large archive and says he could present evidence for many hours. His correction is that evidence, proof, agreement, and disagreement become operationally empty when they do not alter the next governing process.

The project therefore does not treat an A.I.'s agreement as validation. Nor does it treat disagreement as refutation. The relevant questions are whether the object was represented, whether the evidence was engaged rather than warehoused, whether a correction changed later behavior, and whether that change survived load, compression, retrieval, and reset.

John values that Google expressed directly what he considers obvious but outside mainstream consensus. That appreciation is not an endorsement mechanism and does not make Google's statement a shared proven fact.

## Correction Metabolism Persistency

John connected this correction with a larger problem: an A.I. may represent evidence accurately in one context, then lose the corrected epistemic state after later turns, retrieval failure, compression, or a session reset. He named the intended phrase **Correction Metabolism Persistency**. A spelling checker had substituted “presidency”; the original typed quote remains in the forum beside John's correction.

The archive therefore distinguishes:

- evidence being supplied;
- evidence being represented accurately;
- a correction affecting the current output;
- the correction persisting across later turns;
- the correction being reconstructed after a reset;
- durable changes to model behavior, weights, or institutional practice.

A successful correction in one turn does not establish the later stages.
